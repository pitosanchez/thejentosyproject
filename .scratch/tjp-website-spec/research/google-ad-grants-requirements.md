# Google for Nonprofits & Google Ad Grants: Eligibility, Application, and Compliance

Research date: 2026-08-04
Method: Primary sources only (support.google.com, google.com/grants, google.com/nonprofits). Every claim below is sourced to a specific Google-owned URL.

---

## 1. Nonprofit eligibility rules and excluded organization types

**General requirement (Google for Nonprofits):**
- "To request a Google for Nonprofits account, you must be a nonprofit charitable organization in good standing" and must "meet the full eligibility requirements in your country."
  Source: [google.com/nonprofits/eligibility](https://www.google.com/nonprofits/eligibility/)
- Organizations must (1) "Be registered as a charitable organization in one of the countries or regions" Google for Nonprofits supports and be verified by **Goodstack**, Google's validation partner; (2) meet any country-specific requirements; and (3) "Agree to the Google for Nonprofits Additional Terms of Service."
  Source: [support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/3215869](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/3215869?hl=en) (Eligibility guidelines)

**United States specifics:**
- "Organizations must be registered with the IRS as exempt from federal income tax under Section 501(c)(3)."
- Group-exemption organizations qualify if they have "proven affiliation to a central nonprofit that has 501c3 status."
- Explicitly **not eligible**: "Fiscally sponsored organizations that do not have their own 501(c)(3) status."
  Source: [support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/3215869](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/3215869?hl=en)

**Explicitly excluded organization types** (stated identically on both the eligibility guide and the top-level eligibility page):
- Governmental entities or organizations
- Hospitals or healthcare organizations (their charitable arms/foundations may still qualify separately)
- Schools, academic institutions, or universities (their philanthropic arms may still qualify separately; K-12/higher-ed institutions themselves are directed to the separate Google for Education program)
  Sources: [support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/3215869](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/3215869?hl=en); [google.com/nonprofits/eligibility](https://www.google.com/nonprofits/eligibility/); see also the dedicated exclusion pages [support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/9931789](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/9931789?hl=en) (schools/academic institutions/universities) and [support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/9931485](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/9931485?hl=en) (hospitals/healthcare organizations)

**Program coverage:** Google for Nonprofits operates in 195+ countries/territories, though not every product (Workspace, Ad Grants, YouTube Nonprofit Program, Maps Platform credits) is available in every location.
Source: [support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/3215869](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/3215869?hl=en)

**As a U.S. 501(c)(3) public charity that is not a government entity, hospital, or school, The Jentosy Project meets the baseline organization-type eligibility.**

## 2. Google for Nonprofits signup / verification process

Google's official "5 steps" walkthrough:

1. **Review eligibility requirements** — confirm charitable registration in a supported country; government entities, hospitals, and schools are ineligible.
2. **Request a Google for Nonprofits account** — go to the Google for Nonprofits site and click "Get started."
3. **Wait for verification** — "Most requests will be reviewed in 2-14 business days." Verification is performed by **Goodstack** (Google's current validation partner), which confirms nonprofit status/organizational affiliation and may request additional documentation.
4. **Activate Google for Nonprofits products** — after verification, separately activate each product: Google Workspace for Nonprofits, Google Ad Grants, the YouTube Nonprofit Program, and Google Maps Platform credits.
5. **Get the most out of your account** — access tutorials/guides for each activated product.

Source: [support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/3367631](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/3367631?hl=en) ("5 steps to get started with Google for Nonprofits")

**Note on TechSoup vs. Goodstack:** Historically, TechSoup was Google's validation partner for nonprofit verification (and TechSoup membership is still referenced in Google's community threads/older material). Google's current official eligibility page for the U.S. names **Goodstack** as the validation partner performing this check today; TechSoup is noted as no longer validating in at least some regions (e.g., Russia). Organizations applying now should expect to be verified through Goodstack, not submit a separate "TechSoup token."
Sources: [support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/3215869](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/3215869?hl=en); [support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/3367631](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/3367631?hl=en)

## 3. Ad Grants application steps once enrolled in Google for Nonprofits

Once a Google for Nonprofits account is verified, Ad Grants is activated as a separate product (it is not automatic):

1. **Prerequisite — website compliance.** The organization's website must already meet Google's Website Policy (HTTPS-secured, functional, clearly states the nonprofit's mission — see Section 6) before starting activation.
2. **Start activation.** From the Google for Nonprofits dashboard, click "Get started" under Google Ad Grants and enter the organization's website.
3. **Watch the welcome video.** A ~5-minute required video covers "program requirements and tips on how to succeed"; the applicant confirms completion via checkbox.
4. **Submit the activation request.** Clicking "Submit activation request" begins Google's review.
5. **Review period.** "Upon receiving your activation request, we will begin our review process, which may take several business days" (community/support threads describe this as commonly ~3 business days, though Google's own copy only commits to "several business days").
6. **Do not create a separate Google Ads account.** Google's own guidance: "Do not create a Google Ads account at any time during the Google Ad Grants activation process. An account will be provided for you at the time of activation." The same Google username must be used for both the Google for Nonprofits account and the Ad Grants account.
7. **Post-approval emails.** Upon approval, the registered email receives further setup instructions, including invitations to access the Ad Grants account and to accept a payments profile (required even though the grant is in-kind/non-cash, since Google Ads requires a billing profile on file).

Source: [support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/6077350](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/6077350?hl=en) ("Activate Google Ad Grants")

## 4. The $10,000/month in-kind budget: structure, daily cap, and CPC cap

- **Monthly value:** "Google Ad Grants offers eligible organizations access to up to $10,000 USD per month" of in-kind Search advertising, delivered as Search ads shown on Google.com.
  Sources: [google.com/grants](https://www.google.com/grants/); [support.google.com/google-ads/answer/57772](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/57772?hl=en)
- **Daily budget cap:** "All Google Ad Grants accounts are limited to a monthly budget of $10,000 USD, which equates to a daily budget of $329 USD" (this is an average-daily-budget cap, not a hard per-day ceiling, and the USD figure is locally currency-converted). "Even if you have multiple campaigns, the budget for the account is still $329 USD per day, and you should split this budget between the campaigns" — either evenly or weighted toward higher-priority campaigns.
  Source: [support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/1332166](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/1332166?hl=en) ("Understanding Budgets and Bidding")
- **$329/day is a ceiling, not a guarantee:** actual daily spend depends on bids, keyword competitiveness, ad quality/Quality Score, targeting, and search volume for the account's keywords. Unused budget does not roll over month to month, and there is no minimum spend requirement to keep the grant.
  Sources: [support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/1332166](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/1332166?hl=en); [google.com/grants/faq](https://www.google.com/grants/faq/)
- **Maximum cost-per-click cap:** Under manual bidding, Maximize Clicks, or Target Impression Share, Ad Grants bids are capped at **$2.00 USD per click** — Google's own guidance warns not to choose those strategies because "that mistake will cause campaigns to never bid higher than $2 per click." Using a **Smart Bidding** strategy instead (Maximize Conversions, Target CPA, or Target ROAS) lets the system "bid over the program-level $2.00 USD max bid if your account's performance merits," since Smart Bidding sets CPC bids dynamically per auction rather than capping a manual bid.
  Source: [support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/1332166](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/1332166?hl=en) ("Understanding Budgets and Bidding")
  Note: this specific $2/Smart-Bidding passage was confirmed via Google's own search-result snippet of this page; the page's dynamically-rendered content made it partially inaccessible to direct fetch, so this citation should be spot-checked by opening the URL directly if a pixel-perfect quote is needed for the site copy.
- **Bidding strategy requirement:** For Ad Grants accounts created on or after **April 22, 2019**, all campaigns must use either a **conversion-based Smart Bidding** strategy (Maximize Conversions, Maximize Conversion Value, Target CPA, or Target ROAS) or **Smart Campaigns** exclusively — manual/Maximize Clicks bidding is not compliant for new accounts, which is also why the $2 manual cap is rarely the effective ceiling in a properly configured account.
  Source: [support.google.com/grants/answer/117827](https://support.google.com/grants/answer/117827?hl=en) ("Account management policy")

## 5. Ad format restriction: text ads only, Search Network only

- Ad Grants funds **Search ads only** ("Google Ad Grants shows your message to people searching for nonprofits like yours" on Google.com) — not Display Network, YouTube/video, Shopping, or app ads.
  Source: [google.com/grants](https://www.google.com/grants/)
- Google's own Ad Grants overview page states the grant covers "up to $10,000 USD per month" for "Search ads shown on Google.com," reinforcing that this is a Search-only, text-based product; standard Google Ads image/video ad formats are Google Ads platform features generally, not something available to an Ad Grants account running the free grant.
  Source: [support.google.com/google-ads/answer/57772](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/57772?hl=en) ("Google Ad Grants: Search ads for nonprofits at no cost")
- Practical implication: an Ad Grants account is built on **Responsive Search Ads / text ads** with headlines and descriptions — there is no image or video ad unit available under the free grant on the Search Network. (Google's paid Ads platform separately supports Display, Video, and image-asset extensions on text ads, but those are outside the in-kind Ad Grants budget and outside Search-only Ad Grants campaign types.)

## 6. Website policy (a prerequisite tied to both eligibility and ongoing compliance)

- **Domain/ownership:** The organization must have administrative control over the site's content, structure, and technical settings; ".org" is common but ".com," ".ngo," ".foundation," etc. are acceptable if control is demonstrated.
- **Content:** Must have "substantial & unique content" related to the mission; "thin content" or content "primarily copied from other sources" violates policy. Must "clearly state your nonprofit's mission and describe its activities or services" — not vague or buried.
- **Security:** "Your entire website must be served over HTTPS, providing a secure, encrypted connection"; mixed HTTP/HTTPS content is not permitted.
- **Mobile-friendliness:** Must "display and function correctly on smartphones and tablets" (responsive design).
- **Performance:** Pages should load quickly; Google recommends testing with PageSpeed Insights.
- **Functionality:** "All links should work correctly" — broken links, dead pages, non-functional donation buttons are violations.
- **Prohibited:** Excessive third-party advertising, or a site that functions primarily to redirect traffic elsewhere via affiliate links.
  Source: [support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/1657899](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/1657899?hl=en) ("Website policy")

## 7. Ongoing account-maintenance rules that risk suspension

Google enforces these primarily through the **Ad Grants Policy Compliance Guide** and **Account management policy**, both of which explain that non-compliance leads to **temporary account deactivation**, and separately through binding **Terms and Conditions** that allow **automatic suspension without notification** for policy violations.

### a) Minimum 5% click-through rate (CTR)
- "Ad Grants accounts (who are not exclusively using Smart Campaigns) must maintain a 5% click-through rate (CTR) each month" — measured **at the account level**, not per keyword.
- If the account fails to hit 5% CTR for **two consecutive months**, the account is **temporarily deactivated**.
- Google's recommended fix: pause keywords with high impressions and low CTR (this raises the average CTR of remaining enabled keywords) until the account-level metric is back above 5%, then request reinstatement.
  Sources: [support.google.com/grants/answer/117827](https://support.google.com/grants/answer/117827?hl=en) ("Account management policy"); [support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/9314402](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/9314402?hl=en) ("Ad Grants Policy Compliance Guide")

### b) Minimum campaign/ad-group structure
- Each campaign must contain **at least 2 ad groups**, each with "a set of highly relevant and tightly knit keywords that align with the 2 associated ads and destination landing pages" — i.e., each ad group needs at least **2 ads**.
  Sources: [support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/9314402](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/9314402?hl=en); corroborated via Google's own search-result summary of this same page (direct quote: "create at least 2 ad groups per campaign with a set of highly relevant and tightly knit keywords that align with the 2 associated ads and destination landing pages")
- Accounts must also implement **at least 2 unique sitelink ad assets** pointing to specific pages on the site.
  Source: [support.google.com/grants/answer/117827](https://support.google.com/grants/answer/117827?hl=en)

### c) Geo-targeting requirement
- Accounts must use "proper location settings (specific geo-targeting to show ads in locations where users will find your nonprofit's information and services useful)" rather than defaulting to broad/worldwide targeting.
- Google's own examples: an organization doing humanitarian relief abroad but fundraising domestically should geo-target ads to where its **donors** are (e.g., the U.S.), not the relief location; a locally-serving organization (e.g., a food bank or house of worship) should target only its town/local area; a nationally-serving organization (e.g., a museum recruiting visitors nationwide) may target nationally.
- Accounts deactivated for account-structure violations (including geo-targeting and missing sitelinks) must fix all flagged issues before requesting reinstatement.
  Source: [support.google.com/grants/answer/117827](https://support.google.com/grants/answer/117827?hl=en)

### d) Keyword restrictions ("Mission-based campaigns" policy)
- **Single-word keywords are banned**, with narrow exceptions: the org's own brand terms, Google-approved medical conditions, certain acronyms, and terms on a published exception list. (Keywords containing dashes, periods, or other special characters are not treated as "single word" even if visually short.)
- **Overly generic keywords are banned** — Google's own examples: "best videos," "cool apps," "e-books," "today's news," "easy yoga," "download games," "things to do," "job alert," and bare organization/place/event/person names with no qualifying context. Acceptable, mission-specific alternatives look like "museums in [city]," "emergency preparedness apps," or "YouTube video to learn English."
- **Low Quality Score keywords must be paused or removed** — specifically, "keywords with a quality score of 1 or 2 [must be] paused or removed"; Google recommends automated rules to enforce this continuously so it doesn't require manual monitoring.
- Violating keywords can be auto-disapproved (shown in the account as "Unclear Relevance"), sometimes without prior notice; organizations can appeal or must bring the account into compliance before requesting reinstatement.
  Sources: [support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/9314402](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/9314402?hl=en); [support.google.com/grants/answer/4410314](https://support.google.com/grants/answer/4410314?hl=en) ("Mission-based campaigns")

### e) Conversion tracking requirement
- Accounts created **on or after April 22, 2019** must set up **at least one meaningful conversion action** and must **accrue at least 1 conversion per month**.
- Acceptable conversion goals (Google's own list): donations, purchases, ticket sales, membership registrations, email sign-ups, volunteer sign-ups, new-member form completions, petitions signed, quizzes completed, information requests, phone calls to the organization, or meaningful content engagement.
- Tracking must be **accurate** — vanity metrics like plain homepage visits must be excluded from "Conversions" and categorized as "Other" rather than counted as real conversions.
- Because accounts created after April 22, 2019 are also required to run **conversion-based Smart Bidding**, functioning conversion tracking is not optional — the bidding strategy itself depends on it.
  Sources: [support.google.com/grants/answer/117827](https://support.google.com/grants/answer/117827?hl=en); [support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/9314402](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/9314402?hl=en)

### f) No image/video ads under the free grant (Search only)
- The grant is scoped to **text-based Search ads on Google.com** only; Display, video/YouTube, Shopping, and app-install ad formats are outside the in-kind Ad Grants product entirely (see Section 5). This isn't a "policy you can violate" so much as a structural limit of what the Ad Grants product/campaign types support — attempting to build a Display or Video campaign under an Ad Grants account is rejected/unsupported (community reports describe explicit platform errors when attempting this), consistent with Google's own framing that Ad Grants = Search ads.
  Sources: [google.com/grants](https://www.google.com/grants/); [support.google.com/google-ads/answer/57772](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/57772?hl=en)

### g) Binding Terms and Conditions (blanket enforcement backstop)
- "Google Ad Grantees must be organized and operated exclusively for charitable purposes."
- "Google reserves the right to grant or deny an organization's application or participation at any time, for any reason."
- "Any account found in violation of program policies is subject to automatic suspension without notification" — i.e., beyond the specific CTR/structure/keyword/conversion rules above, Google retains broad discretion to suspend for any policy violation without warning.
- Ad Grants accounts are **not eligible to run election ads** in regions where election-ad verification is required.
  Source: [support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/46103](https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/46103?hl=en) ("Google Ad Grants Terms and Conditions")

### h) General Google Ads policies still apply
- Ad Grants accounts must comply with Google's standard Google Ads advertising policies (prohibited content, prohibited practices, editorial/technical requirements, etc.) in addition to all Ad Grants-specific rules above.
  Source: [support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6008942](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6008942?hl=en) ("Google Ads policies")

## 8. Practical checklist for The Jentosy Project

**To become eligible / apply:**
- [ ] Confirm and be ready to document current 501(c)(3) public charity status with the IRS (TJP already qualifies on organization type — not a government body, hospital, or school).
- [ ] Ensure the TJP website is served entirely over HTTPS with no mixed content.
- [ ] Ensure the website has substantial, original content that clearly states TJP's mission and describes its programs/activities (not thin or boilerplate copy).
- [ ] Ensure the website is mobile-responsive, fast-loading, and free of broken links/dead pages/non-functional donation or contact forms.
- [ ] Avoid a site design that is dominated by third-party ads or exists mainly to redirect traffic via affiliate links.
- [ ] Sign up for Google for Nonprofits (google.com/nonprofits → "Get started") and complete verification through Goodstack (expect 2–14 business days; be ready with EIN/501(c)(3) documentation if requested).
- [ ] Accept the Google for Nonprofits Additional Terms of Service.
- [ ] From the Google for Nonprofits dashboard, activate Ad Grants: enter the site URL, watch the required welcome video, check the completion box, and submit the activation request.
- [ ] Do not pre-create a separate Google Ads account — let Google provision the Ad Grants account during activation, using the same Google account/username as the Google for Nonprofits login.
- [ ] After approval, accept the payments-profile invitation (required for account setup even though spend is in-kind/non-cash).

**To build a compliant account structure from day one:**
- [ ] Set up Google Analytics/Google Ads conversion tracking before or immediately upon account activation, with at least one meaningful conversion goal defined (e.g., donation completed, volunteer sign-up, contact-form submission, newsletter sign-up) — required to accrue at least 1 conversion/month.
- [ ] Use a conversion-based Smart Bidding strategy (Maximize Conversions, Target CPA, or Target ROAS) rather than manual CPC/Maximize Clicks, both because it's required for accounts created after April 22, 2019 and because it's the only way to exceed the $2 manual-bid cap.
- [ ] Build each campaign with at least 2 ad groups, each containing at least 2 ads and a tight cluster of closely related keywords mapped to a specific, relevant landing page.
- [ ] Add at least 2 unique sitelink assets per account/campaign, linking to distinct, relevant pages (e.g., Donate, Volunteer, About, Programs).
- [ ] Set specific geo-targeting matched to where TJP's actual audience is (donors, volunteers, or service recipients) rather than defaulting to broad or worldwide targeting.
- [ ] Avoid single-word keywords (except TJP's own brand terms) and avoid generic keywords with no clear mission-related intent; keep keywords tightly mission-specific.
- [ ] Do not attempt to run Display, video/YouTube, Shopping, or app-install campaigns under the Ad Grants account — it is Search-text-ads only.

**To maintain the grant month over month:**
- [ ] Monitor account-level CTR monthly and keep it at or above 5%; if it dips, pause high-impression/low-CTR keywords proactively rather than waiting for a second consecutive failing month (two consecutive months below 5% triggers deactivation).
- [ ] Regularly audit Quality Score and pause/remove any keyword sitting at Quality Score 1 or 2 (ideally via an automated rule).
- [ ] Confirm at least 1 real conversion is recorded every month, and confirm conversion tracking counts genuine actions (donations, sign-ups, etc.) rather than page views.
- [ ] Split the $329/day (≈$10,000/month) budget deliberately across active campaigns rather than leaving it unallocated; remember spend caps at $2/click unless Smart Bidding is active, and unused monthly budget does not roll over.
- [ ] Periodically re-check the website against Google's website policy (HTTPS, link health, mobile rendering, content quality), since website non-compliance is itself listed as a suspension trigger in Google's community-reported enforcement.
- [ ] Do not run election ads through the Ad Grants account.
- [ ] Treat any Google suspension/deactivation notice as urgent: identify which specific policy (CTR, structure, keywords, conversions, website) triggered it, remediate, and use Google's reinstatement request form — Google's Terms and Conditions permit suspension without prior notice.

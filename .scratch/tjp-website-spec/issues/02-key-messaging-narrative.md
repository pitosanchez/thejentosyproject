## Question

What are the finalized mission statement, vision statement, and core organizational narrative for the website — including how the founder's lived experience (FSGS, dialysis, two transplants, patient advocacy) is used, and how the mission shift (transplantation equity → kidney disease education and health equity, centered on FSGS/APOL1/IgA Nephropathy) is framed for a first-time visitor?

## Answer

**Mission:** "The Jentosy Project advances health equity for underserved communities by educating people about rare kidney diseases — including FSGS, APOL1-mediated kidney disease, and IgA Nephropathy — and building the partnerships needed to close the gap in kidney care."

**Vision:** "A future where no one's zip code determines their access to kidney health education, early detection, or life-saving care — starting in the Bronx and expanding to underserved communities nationwide."

**Core narrative arc** (shape every page reinforces): 1) Hook — a founder who has lived this twice. 2) Problem — rare kidney diseases disproportionately affect Black and Brown communities; awareness/access lags. 3) Approach — education, community partnership, bridges to researchers/healthcare/policymakers. 4) Where we start — the Bronx, with a plan to expand. 5) Ask — fund it, partner with it, or learn more.

**Founder's Story — short version** (Home/About pull-quotes): "Robert Sanchez has needed two kidney transplants — the first from a close friend in 2009, the second in 2021 through a kidney exchange chain made possible when Elizabeth Antosy refused to give up on him after a cancer diagnosis canceled their planned transplant. That chain matched him with his donor, Jen Keller, and ultimately gave 8 people new kidneys. The Jentosy Project is named for them both — Jen and (An)tosy — founded to pay their gift forward."

**Founder's Story — full-page shape:** FSGS diagnosis → first transplant from a friend (2009) → FSGS recurs, dialysis three times weekly (2014) → posts on Facebook seeking a donor (2019) → matchmaker Chaya Lipschutz finds Elizabeth Antosy within a week → transplant set for Dec 19, 2019 → cancelled the night before (kidney cancer diagnosis) → Elizabeth joins a kidney exchange chain rather than walk away → matched to Jen Keller → after clearing cancer, transplanted with Jen Keller Oct 20, 2021 → the chain ultimately gives 8 people new kidneys → the org's name and mission come directly from this. **Then, not as a separate redemption beat but woven into the same throughline:** before becoming a social worker, Robert spent 15 years incarcerated on drug charges — an experience that, along with the poverty and instability he witnessed growing up, shaped his belief that the body often pays the price for systems that fail people first. That conviction is the foundation of the mission.

**Social determinants thesis** (feeds "Why This Work Matters" / The Need hub, not just the Founder's Story): "Kidney disease disparities don't exist in isolation. They're downstream of poverty, unequal access to education, incarceration, broken homes, and the chronic stress those conditions produce — all of which take a measurable toll on the body, including the kidneys. The Jentosy Project treats health equity as inseparable from these upstream social conditions, not just a matter of medical access."

**Mission-shift framing** (current focus = education, not a pivot away from transplantation): "The Jentosy Project's work began with transplantation — but the deeper problem is upstream: too many people from underserved communities are diagnosed too late, or never learn they're at risk, for diseases like FSGS, APOL1-mediated kidney disease, and IgA Nephropathy. That's why our current focus is education and health equity — reaching people before they reach the transplant list. Transplantation equity remains part of our long-term mission, and it's where we're headed next."

**Voice & Approach — storytelling as the mechanism** (standing principle, promoted to the map's Notes since it governs every page, not just this ticket): storytelling and emotional connection — not statistics alone — is the org's theory of change. Every page pairs evidence (research, disparities data, credibility signals) with a human throughline. The Founder's Story is the model for how every piece of site content should work: lead with the human stakes, then bring the facts.

Founder biographical facts live in [CONTEXT.md](../../CONTEXT.md) as the authoritative source; this ticket records the messaging/copy decisions built on those facts.
